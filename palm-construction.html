Mulligan Funding: why $1.5MM is solid, $2MM is conditional, and why we can’t go higher

Offers 3–5 ($1,500,000) are approved and ready to close on the documents below. Offers 6–8 ($2,000,000) are Mulligan Funding’s maximum. Beyond the underwriting call every offer needs, they also require final credit committee sign-off, and the amount could be reduced at that stage. We pushed for the $2.5MM / 26-month structure; Mulligan Funding declined for these reasons:

  • Cost vs. profit during the expansion. At $2.5MM over 26 months the total payback would be about $3.225MM. That ~$700K cost of capital would likely absorb most of the company’s profit over the term, since the cover letter notes new locations take about two years to turn a profit.
  • Pace of growth. Going from 4 to 8 locations in roughly 10 months on a $3–4MM need. The lender wants to start at $1.5–2MM and increase through top-ups as the new locations come online.
  • High volume, thin margins. The consolidated financials show about $51MM in revenue YTD versus $41.7MM through August in the cover letter; the lender reads much of the G&H income as pass-through rather than margin.
  • Open litigation. Lawsuits are common at this volume, but a couple of the current suits allege subcontractors went unpaid, which the lender flagged against the personal guarantor.
  • Cash-basis accounting makes profitability harder for the lender to verify.

The path to more: Mulligan Funding is not saying no to growth. It sees a real opportunity to increase the amount over time and simply doesn’t want to get too aggressive up front. Close now, perform on the payments, and top-ups can grow alongside the expansion as each new location comes online.
