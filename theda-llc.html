OnDeck and Mulligan offer each term at two prices. The lower-rate option forgives part of the remaining interest if you pay off early (25% with OnDeck, 50% with Mulligan) and carries a 2.5% origination fee. The higher-rate option waives 100% of remaining interest on early payoff and carries a 4% origination fee. The lower rate is the better choice if you plan to hold the money for the full term; 100% forgiveness is the better choice if you plan to pay off early — and it gives you more flexibility.
Rapid Finance charges no origination fee at all — the full amount is deposited. Its early-payoff benefit works differently: repay the loan in full inside the window for that term (4 months on the 12-month, 5 months on the 15-month, 6 months on the 18-month) and 20% of the loan’s fixed fee is discounted. That amendment is added to the financing agreement automatically.
On the OnDeck and Mulligan offers, the crossed-out origination fee is the lender’s standard fee and the figure beneath it is the fee after the 1% September reimbursement from BusinessLoans.com. Rapid Finance charges no origination fee, so there is nothing to reimburse there.
Either way, all of these options are true business loans that report to commercial credit — and because they are loans rather than merchant cash advances, the interest is tax deductible (consult a tax professional).
